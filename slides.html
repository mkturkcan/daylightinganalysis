Statute

What the law requires

Definition. Daylighting is the removal of parking beside a crosswalk, so that a driver approaching the corner and a pedestrian stepping into it can see each other.

  1. New York City is exempt from the New York State prohibition on parking within 20 feet of a crosswalk, so daylighting in the city exists only where signage or a physical construction designates it.
  2. Local Law 66 of 2023, at Administrative Code section 19-175.8, required the Department of Transportation to do three things: (i) publish a report assessing the feasibility of daylighting, which is the January 2025 report, (ii) daylight at least 100 intersections annually from 1 January 2025, and (iii) publish a progress report each year. Subsection (g) begins location level reporting with calendar 2025.
  3. The agency committed to install 1000 daylighting improvements in 2024, above the 100 the law sets as a floor.
  4. California AB 413, which the January 2025 report records as passed in 2023 and taking effect in 2025, makes a no parking zone of 20 feet standard across that state, reduced to 15 feet where a locality installs curb extensions, and prohibits parking there for motor vehicles rather than for bicycles or scooters.
  5. New York State law prohibits parking within 15 feet of a fire hydrant, which is 4.572 m, so a corner carrying a hydrant was already clear before any programme reached it.

The report also records the geometry the guidance asks for: around 20 to 30 feet of daylighting is often recommended.

Before this study

The evidence before this study

The January 2025 report states the position in its own literature review: there is “little direct evidence connecting daylighting to safety”, and the practice is “frequently justified based on past literature and practices rather than empirical research”.

  1. The four endorsements. The report's endorsements are guidance documents rather than evaluations: (i) the Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices, (ii) the Federal Highway Administration's 2018 guide to uncontrolled crossing locations, (iii) the National Association of City Transportation Officials urban street design guide, and (iv) a National Cooperative Highway Research Program guide. A guidance document recommends a practice; it does not measure one.
  2. The four case study cities. Seattle, Hoboken, San Francisco and Portland have each implemented daylighting at scale, and none has published a before and after analysis of its safety effect. San Francisco reported a decrease of 14 per cent in collisions at the 80 intersections daylit in the Tenderloin in 2018, with no data available on injury reductions.

No published analysis of daylighting carries a comparison group, a significance test and published locations at the same time. The January 2025 report has a comparison group and a significance test and publishes no locations. The Safety Treatment Evaluation it relies on has neither a comparison group nor a significance test. The case study cities have published nothing.

The nearest independent measurement is Kumfer and others 2026, report DDOT-RDT-26-02, on 47 curb extensions in Washington DC. Its outcomes are driver yielding and pedestrian satisfaction, collected by survey and video rather than from crash records, so it measures behaviour at the corner rather than injury counts.

One corner

Broadway and West 45 Street

A curb regulation sign order recorded as daylighting, installed 2019-01 at the south west corner, 10.5 m from the centre of the junction. The outcome is all reported crashes within 30 m. The window is 36 months before installation and 24 months after, with the installation month excluded. The comparison is 3 matched intersections.

  1. Counts. 111 crashes in the 36 months before, 40 in the 24 months after.
  2. Annual rates at the corner. 12 × 111 / 36 = 37.0 crashes per year before, and 12 × 40 / 24 = 20.0 after.
  3. Change at the corner. 20.0 − 37.0 = −17.0 crashes per year.
  4. The comparison intersections. The 3 of them average 63.0 crashes over the before window and 20.0 over the after window, which is 21.0 and 10.0 per year, so their mean change is −11.0 crashes per year.
  5. The difference. −17.0 − (−11.0) = −6.0 crashes per year.

Crashes fell at the treated corner and they also fell at its comparison intersections over the very same calendar months, so the estimate for this corner is the extra fall of 6.0 crashes per year rather than the whole fall of 17.0.

The same corner against the 4 corridor comparison locations the published rule selects gives 17.5 crashes per year before and 5.625 after, a mean change of −11.875, and a difference of −5.125. Both figures come from the per site sums the companion pages ship, so a reader can recompute either one.

The estimate is the unweighted mean over treated sites of the change at the site minus the mean change at that site's own comparison locations, tested against zero by a two sided one sample t test on n minus 1 degrees of freedom. Every treated site enters with weight one over n whatever its crash count, so the quantity estimated is the change at the mean treated corner, in injuries per intersection per year, and zero means no effect.

Changing one component at a time identifies the component that carries the disagreement. On the city's own cohort of 2019 to 2021, on the same 257 turn calming corners, the corridor rule gives −0.2389 (p = 0.003) on pedestrian injuries and the matched rule gives −0.0136 (p = 0.850); under fixed effects Poisson the same pair of comparison rules gives 0.7832 (p = 0.0010) and 1.0563 (p = 0.397). Within a column the two estimators agree with each other, and across columns they disagree together, so the comparison group is the responsible component and the estimator does not change the answer.

Design

How the three specifications differ

  1. What changes between them. Specification 2 changes three components of specification 1: (i) the crash record, which runs to the full record, (ii) the installation window, which runs to eight years, and (iii) the before period, which is 36 months rather than 24. It then runs over every subset. Specification 3 changes the comparison group and the estimator as well, and it adds a placebo on every cell together with Benjamini and Hochberg adjustment within each outcome.
  2. How the comparison intersections are built. A treated unit enters a coarse exact stratum on four keys: borough, approach count clipped to 3 to 6, posted speed bucketed at 25, 30 and above 30, and the major leg flag. The three pool members of that stratum closest to it in Mahalanobis distance become its comparison intersections. The distance uses four numbers per unit, all crashes and pedestrian crashes within 30 m annualised over months −36 to −25 and again over months −24 to −13, so the final twelve months before installation are withheld from the matching. Every pool member carries no treatment within 30 m and sits at least 300 m from every treated unit of the subset.

Reading the five device rows

  1. The largest row is 134 Citi Bike docks, 70.9 per cent of the locations, and it is not significant on its own at p = 0.08. A dock is sited on ridership demand.
  2. The only individually significant row is the 14 neckdowns at p = 0.02. Conclusion, published specification

What the reproduction settles

  1. The computation is correct. The published values fall inside our confidence intervals in 6 of 6 comparable estimates and agree in sign in all 6. Nothing in this study contradicts the report's arithmetic.
  2. The reported reduction would be found again. Run on the city's crash record with the city's comparison rule, the pedestrian injury estimate for hardened daylighting is a reduction of 0.14 per intersection per year, and it is significant.
  3. Three properties of the sample limit what it can mean. 70.9 per cent of the significant group is Citi Bike stations, individually not significant; the only individually significant component is 7.4 per cent neckdowns, which are curb reconstructions; and no component is the posts or granite blocks the report defines the treatment as.
  4. A reader who stops here should conclude that the city measured a reduction in the group it assembled, and that the group is not the treatment the recommendation is about.

Falsification 1 of 3

The placebo test

The estimate is recomputed with one change: the installation month of every treated site is moved back in time, far enough that the whole analysis window falls in months before anything was built. The corners, the comparison locations, the estimator and the outcome all stay as they were. Because no treatment exists anywhere in that window, the honest answer is no effect.

s=max(pre,post+1)
s \;=\; \max\!\left(\text{pre},\; \text{post}+1\right)
The placebo displacement, chosen so the placebo window ends before installation.
LaTeX sources \;=\; \max\!\left(\text{pre},\; \text{post}+1\right)

Concrete case. Under a before period of 24 months and an after period of 24 months, moving the installation month back by 24 puts the final month of the placebo window on the installation month itself, which is partly treated. Taking the larger of 24 and 25 gives a displacement of 25 months, so the placebo window runs from month −49 to month −1 and every month of it lies strictly before installation.

Falsification 2 of 3

The admission condition

A cell is read only when the placebo is consistent with no effect.

θ^plac=θ^(ti→ti−s), pplac≥0.05
\hat\theta^{\text{plac}} \;=\; \hat\theta\big(t_i \to t_i - s\big), \qquad \text{admit the cell only if } p^{\text{plac}} \ge 0.05
The placebo estimate and the condition a cell must satisfy to be read.
LaTeX source\hat\theta^{\text{plac}} \;=\; \hat\theta\big(t_i \to t_i - s\big), \qquad \text{admit the cell only if } p^{\text{plac}} \ge 0.05

Three conditions hold together before a cell is read as a finding: (i) the placebo p value is at least 0.05, (ii) the Benjamini and Hochberg adjusted p value falls below 0.05, and (iii) at least 25 treated units contribute. A cell is refused as well when the compounded pre treatment drift accounts for 60 per cent or more of the estimate, and it is marked underpowered when the smallest detectable effect exceeds 25 per cent. A cell that fails any of these is reported with the reason rather than dropped.

The published crash record cannot support this test on its own installation window. A placebo with a before period of 24 months needs months 48 to 1 before installation. That record starts 2017-01 and the earliest installations are 2019-01, so across the installation window 79 per cent of the months a placebo requires are present. A month with no crash record contributes zero on the treated side and zero on the comparison side, which pulls the placebo toward zero, and all six affected placebo values were positive, which is the direction that reports no problem.

Repairing that crash floor and changing nothing else leaves five of the six published estimates clear of the placebo. The exception is turn calming pedestrian injuries, where the repaired placebo is −0.206 (p = 0.022) against an estimate of −0.236, so a specification containing no treatment reproduces 87 per cent of that estimate.

Falsification 3 of 3

Why is a decrease at p below 0.01 not evidence about the treatment?

Suppose a cell returns a decrease at p below 0.01, and the placebo on months containing no treatment returns a decrease of similar size. Both cannot have been caused by the treatment, because in the placebo window there was no treatment, so the specification produces decreases at these corners whether or not anything is installed.

  1. A decrease with a failing placebo. Turn calming pooled, pedestrian injuries, 975 treated units under the published specification on the extended record: −0.272 per intersection per year at p = 5×10−12, and a placebo of −0.135 at p = 0.0019. A window containing no treatment reproduces half of the estimate.
  2. An increase with a failing placebo. Hardened daylighting at a junction, 143 treated units under the falsification tested specification: a rate ratio of 1.153 [0.954, 1.394] on pedestrian injuries, with a placebo of 0.747 at p = 0.0006. A specification that produces a decrease of 25 per cent from no treatment cannot be trusted to have produced 1.153 from the treatment.

The p value answers whether a result could have arisen by chance. The placebo answers whether the specification produces that result without a treatment, and those are different questions. A refused cell is evidence of no effect and it is evidence of an effect in neither direction; it is a cell in which the design cannot separate the treatment from the reason the site was chosen.

  1. What each fixed effect absorbs. The unit effect absorbs everything about a corner that does not change over the record: its traffic volume, its number of approaches, its posted speed, its geometry and its long run crash level. The calendar month effect absorbs a citywide shock common to that month: weather, a change in police reporting practice, congestion pricing from 2025-01, and the citywide crash trend.

The month at event time zero is excluded, because an installation month is partly treated. Inference uses the CRV1 cluster robust variance estimator with standard errors clustered by match group, in which a match group is a treated unit together with its own three matched comparison intersections. Because the specification weights a unit by its expected count, high exposure corners dominate the estimate and the quantity is the proportional change in the total count, whereas the published estimator gives the change at the mean corner. The two answer different questions and their magnitudes are not comparable: no effect is 1 on the ratio scale and 0 on the difference scale.

Falsification tested specification

Results over 240 cells

  1. 240 cells, being 40 estimable subsets on 6 outcomes. The admission rule is applied to every one: at least 25 treated units, significance after Benjamini and Hochberg within the outcome, and a placebo consistent with no effect.
  2. 117 of the 235 cells carrying a placebo fail it. The failing placebos are one sided, toward fewer crashes before installation, which is the signature of comparison groups that regress to the mean.
  3. 39 cells are significant before adjustment and 14 after. 13 of those 14 carry a placebo that rejects no effect.
  4. The single cell that survives everything is an increase. Pedestrian injuries in the 2025 sign only cohort, 2.409 with interval 1.263 to 4.595 on 107 units, over 12 months of follow up and with a smallest detectable effect of 60 per cent; the same design produces false increases on 78 per cent of null draws under selection on a persistent level, so this is reported as a property of the design rather than as harm.
  5. No reduction survives. 7 cells do not identify and 18 hold fewer than 25 units. The hardened daylighting cells fail the placebo test and are reported for completeness rather than as findings.

A cell counts as a significant reduction when its estimate lies below no effect at p under 0.05 without adjustment. The single reduction under the falsification tested specification is the sign only arm of the January to August 2024 list on vehicle to vehicle injury crashes, 0.634 [0.430, 0.935] at p = 0.021, and its Benjamini and Hochberg adjusted p value is 0.609, so it does not survive the adjustment either.

A rate ratio and a difference in counts per intersection per year are never comparable in magnitude, because no effect is 1 on one scale and 0 on the other. The two columns can be compared on direction and on significance, and they cannot be ordered or averaged together.

Conclusion, extended record

What the extended record settles

  1. The published design finds reductions almost everywhere it looks. Held fixed and applied to 40 subsets on 6 outcomes, it returns 76 significant reductions out of 233 estimable cells.
  2. It finds them in places no mechanism connects. The same design returns a significant reduction for paint only turn calming and for cohorts that are lists of locations rather than treatments, at similar magnitudes to the hardened estimate.
  3. Its own placebo rejects. Displace every installation date into pre treatment months and re estimate: the design still reports a significant reduction, on months when no work had been done. That is a property of the design rather than of any treatment.
  4. A reader who stops here should conclude that a reduction from this design is not evidence about a treatment, because the design produces one when the treatment is absent.

Each attains approximately the nominal rejection rate in one regime of site selection and rejects far too often in the other, and each design's bias runs in the direction of the estimate that design reports. The city's design, on corridor comparisons, reports a benefit, and a corridor rule produces false benefits under spike selection. This study's design, on matched comparisons, reports harm at the hardened corners, and matching produces false harm under level selection. Neither result is evidence about daylighting until the mixture of transient and persistent in the city's selection is known, and that mixture cannot be established from the published record, because the city has published neither the list it selected from nor the vintage of the crash data it selected on.

Instrument test

What the calibration establishes

  1. Selection on a transient spike. A corridor comparison produces a false benefit on 34 to 88 per cent of draws in every window, because it balances location and nothing else, so it cannot absorb a spike at the treated corner however that spike is timed. A matched comparison gives 4.0 per cent in the window the matcher balances, months −36 to −12, and 46.9 per cent in the final year the matcher deliberately withholds.
  2. Selection on a persistent level. The corridor cells are exactly sized at 5.0 per cent and recover the truth, 0.8005 against a true 0.80. The matched cells reject on 57 and 78 per cent of null draws, and the matched Poisson pair returns 1.0745 when the truth is 0.80, so it reports an increase where the truth is a reduction of a fifth.

Confounding at the corner

Curb already cleared by a hydrant or a bus stop

The observation belongs to the agency. Its January 2025 report contains a hydrant zone analysis, defines a hydrant zone as hydrants and bus stops, and reports a normalised injury rate 30 per cent higher at 7558 such intersections out of 20543 eligible, of which 90 per cent are fire hydrants and 10 per cent bus stops.

That analysis is cross sectional. It compares intersections that have a hydrant zone against those that do not, so it cannot separate the effect of the cleared curb from whatever caused a hydrant to be sited there, and the report's own footnote records that hydrant zone locations average 0.82 injuries per intersection per year, which is low.

  1. A test inside the treated arm. 109725 hydrants and 13618 distinct bus stops are held with their positions. At the working radius of 12 m, 20.5 per cent of daylighted corners at a junction were already clear, against 16.6 per cent of intersections carrying no device. The test here splits the treated arm by whether the corner was already clear and compares treated corners against other treated corners, so it does not rest on a treated against untreated comparison at all.
  2. The registered prediction. Less changed at a corner that was already clear, so that arm should move less. All seven contrasts run in the predicted direction. Daylighting at a junction: 1.099 where the corner was already clear against 0.920 where it was newly cleared, on 426 and 1584 units, z = 1.80, p = 0.071. Turn calming: 1.191 against 0.884, on 90 and 773 units, z = 2.65, p = 0.008.

Study design

What would settle the question

  1. The published location list. The 756 locations behind the January 2025 report are not published, so a reproduction of that specification is a reproduction of a method rather than of a sample. The hardened arm rebuilt here holds 84 e scooter corrals, 15 rubber speed bumps and 4 bicycle islands against their 134 Citi Bike stations, 3 bicycle corrals, 33 turn calming projects, 14 neckdowns and 5 enhanced crossings. Publishing the list closes that gap at no cost.
  2. A comparison group not selected on lagged outcomes. Matching on lagged crash counts is protective when selection acts on a transient component and harmful when it acts on a persistent one, and a corridor rule behaves in the opposite way for the same reason. Both biases are properties of choosing comparisons from the outcome history. A design that selects its comparison group on crash history should at minimum report the achieved exposure of that group beside its estimate; neither design here did.
  3. A staggered rollout with a published eligibility list. Local Law 66 obliges at least 100 intersections a year, so the eligible set is larger than the set treated in any one year. Publishing the eligibility list and the order of treatment lets the comparison group be defined before the outcome is observed rather than matched to it afterwards.

Two repairs cost nothing and are worth stating separately. A crash window should extend at least one before period earlier than the earliest installation analysed, so that a placebo is computable at all. And the eleven turn calming classes, together with what was installed at the 907 records that do not say, are already recorded inside the agency; releasing that field makes the class testable.

The obvious objection

Is the placebo excluding everything because it is broken?

  1. The pattern demands the question. 117 of 235 cells with a placebo fail it, and every reduction that clears multiplicity control fails it. A test that excludes almost everything is either detecting a real problem or malfunctioning.
  2. Evidence that it detects a real problem. The placebo estimates are large and one sided: 0.554 for the posts and blocks programme, 0.672 for pooled hardened daylighting, 0.770 for turn calming. A malfunction would scatter around 1. A pre treatment trend toward fewer crashes at treated corners produces exactly this.
  3. Evidence that the test is too strict. It is a significance test, so on 235 cells with samples in the thousands it rejects on trends too small to matter. A placebo of 0.97 that is significant excludes a cell over a 3 per cent pre trend.
  4. Both are true, and they are separable. The size of the placebo departure distinguishes them, and no current output reports the exclusions ranked by that size.

Source: data/subset_estimates.csv placebo_irr, placebo_p over 235 cells with a placebo.

Further analysis

Six analyses that would separate the two readings

  1. Report the placebo as a magnitude rather than a verdict. Rank every excluded cell by how far its placebo sits from 1, and read the cells whose placebo is within a stated tolerance. A cell with a placebo of 0.98 and a cell with a placebo of 0.55 are not the same evidence.
  2. Estimate the pre treatment trend directly. Fit event time coefficients over months minus 36 to minus 1 and test their slope. A flat pre trend with a significant placebo indicts the placebo; a sloped one indicts the comparison group.
  3. Match without using lagged outcomes. Match on borough, geometry, and traffic volume alone. Matching on crash history is what induces regression to the mean, so a design that avoids it has a placebo with no mechanical reason to reject.
  4. Use the never treated as the comparison group. Compare treated corners against corners that were never treated in any year rather than against corners matched to them, and absorb selection with borough by month effects.
  5. Exploit the eligibility list. Local Law 66 requires at least 100 intersections a year, chosen from a stated priority set. Corners on the priority set that were not yet reached are the natural comparison group, and they are selected the same way the treated corners were.
  6. Calibrate the placebo itself. Simulate under a known null with a known pre trend and record how often the placebo rejects. That gives the placebo a false positive rate, which is the number needed to decide whether 117 of 235 is too many.

Each is a change to src/stats_subsets.py or src/matching.py; none requires data the project does not hold.

Further analysis

What the results look like with the placebo test switched off

  1. The statistical companion carries the switch. Turning the placebo requirement off in analysis.html leaves multiplicity control and the unit floor in place, and shows what the study would report under the admission rule the published analysis used, which contains no placebo.
  2. Off, the matched Poisson design reports 14 significant cells. Of those, 13 have a placebo that rejects no effect, and 1 does not.
  3. Off, the reported directions reverse the published story. The significant cells under this design are increases rather than reductions, including 1.439 for the posts and blocks programme on pedestrian crashes.
  4. That is why the switch is a diagnostic rather than a setting. Neither position gives a defensible estimate: on, nothing survives; off, what survives is an increase from a design that produces false increases at 78 per cent of null draws under the selection these corners display.
